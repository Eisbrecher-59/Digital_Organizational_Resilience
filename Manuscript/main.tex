% Abstract
\begin{abstract}
\vspace*{4mm}
Resilience has become a central concern in management and information systems as organizations confront increasingly complex digital disruptions. Despite its prominence in policy, standards, and organizational discourse, resilience often has remained highly rhetorical and little operational: Conceptual ambiguity, weak operationalization, and fragmented empirical evidence hinder the development of proper organizational foundations for resilience, which in turn hinders the deployment of actionable practices. This paper addresses these challenges through a structured, cross-disciplinary review of resilience research across management, information systems, and related fields. We identified three core limitations in the current understanding of digital resilience in organizations: weak construct clarity, insufficient indicators capable of capturing adaptive performance when disruptions occur, and limited accumulation and exploitation of evidence that would help measure resilience and act upon across organizational contexts. Here, we recognize that digital organizational resilience shall be an enacted socio-technical capability linking organizational routines, technological infrastructures, and ecosystem interactions. We further outline a research agenda that emphasizes early-warning systems, integrative design principles, measurable behavioral indicators, and the importance of combining field data and simulations. This study contributes to theory development and provides a foundation for developing observable and actionable forms of resilience in complex digital environments.
\end{abstract}
