\label{sec:Enactment-Scope}

\subsubsection{Three lines of work, one missing synthesis}

Resilience research has matured along three distinguishable but partial lines, and naming the differences between them clarifies what an integrative framework still has to do. The first line examines digital infrastructures and technological systems: how architectures, redundancy mechanisms, and reconfiguration capacities sustain services during disturbance. Cyber-resilience research provides empirically grounded accounts of detection, response, and recovery \autocite{linkov2013resilience,bjorck2015cyber,dupont2019cyberresilience,hausken2020cyber}; network analysis characterizes topological robustness, scale-free vulnerabilities, and universal recovery dynamics \autocite{albert2000error,gao2016universal,panteli2017metrics}; and formal models of attack--defense dynamics quantify trade-offs between security investment and exposure \autocite{maillart2017bugbounty,aydin2018integration,kuypers2018designing,ibnehossain2020modeling}. This work treats organizations primarily as operators of technical systems. The second line approaches resilience as an organizational capability, foregrounding leadership, mindfulness, governance, and learning routines: from Weick and Sutcliffe's high-reliability tradition \autocite{vogus2007organizational,weick2011managing} to capability- and dynamic-capability-oriented accounts \autocite{teece2007explicating,bhamra2011resilience,lengnick2011developing,duchek2019organizational,boh2023building}. It offers detailed accounts of internal mechanisms but is lighter on the infrastructures through which those mechanisms now operate. The third line addresses the ecosystem level, examining how information sharing, platform governance, and collaborative incident response generate collective adaptive capacity \autocite{arnold2004informationsharing,janssen2006network,enisa2017isacs,liu2022network,lostri2022shared,gillard2023efficient}; it captures coordinated action across organizational boundaries but does less to connect ecosystem-level dynamics back to internal mechanisms.\\

Section~\ref{sec:RG} showed that resilience stalls when concept, evidence, and method remain misaligned. The three literature lines above reproduce that stall in research architecture: technical work under-specifies governance and learning; organizational work under-specifies the infrastructures and partners through which routines now run; ecosystem work under-connects collective action to internal mechanisms. Field interviews report the same pattern in practice---cross-unit coordination breaks down, compliance substitutes for adaptation, and exercises rarely change operational routines \autocite{rota2024}. Closing the gap therefore requires more than another definition or a single-domain maturity model. It requires a specification of \emph{how} adaptive capacity is enacted and \emph{where} those enactments depend on one another across a socio-technical organization.\\

The Enactment--Scope framework is designed to provide that specification. Its \emph{enactment} dimension distinguishes three mechanisms through which resilience is realized---technological, managerial, and collaborative---mapping directly onto the three partial literatures above. Its \emph{scope} dimension locates those mechanisms across infrastructural, organizational, and ecosystem coordination boundaries. Crossing the two dimensions yields a 3$\times$3 grid that asks not only whether a resilience initiative exists, but whether mechanisms in one cell are supported or undermined by dependencies in others. That structure explains a recurring practical failure mode: initiatives that appear strong in isolation---mature SOC operations, cloud redundancy, Information Sharing and Analysis Center (ISAC) membership---still produce weak adaptive performance when the coupled cells remain thin, not least because security information sharing depends on behavioral participation that often remains reluctant even among ISAC members \autocite{mermoud2019share,skopik2016problem}.\\

The framework separates two tasks that policy and practice often conflate. The first is \emph{translation}: moving from articulated resilience---strategy language, audit artifacts, board reporting---to enacted capability observable under disruption. Figure~\ref{fig:triangle} models that translation through three mechanisms already prominent in standards and governance discourse: operational indicators that render adaptive behavior visible; design principles that allocate decision rights and structure coordination; and benchmarks and warning systems that align incentives and signal departure from desired states \autocite{linkov2013resilience,boin2013resilient,francis2014metric,skopik2016problem,bcbs2021operational,nist2024csf}. The second task is \emph{placement}: locating those translation mechanisms---and the underlying technological, managerial, and collaborative practices they govern---in the socio-technical settings where organizations actually coordinate. Figure~\ref{fig:enactment_scope} (introduced below) maps concrete practices into the enactment--scope grid. Together, the two figures specify what must be translated and where translation must land if resilience is to become governable and revisable as conditions evolve, rather than merely declarative.\\

\subsection{Integrating Enactment and Scope}

\input{figures_and_tables/Enactment-Scope}

Figure~\ref{fig:enactment_scope} renders the framework as a 3$\times$3 grid that locates concrete resilience mechanisms at their socio-technical address. Field interviews \autocite{rota2024} illustrate a recurring coupling problem: even within the organizational scope, IT, risk, and compliance units often operate in silos that constrain how internal mechanisms combine, let alone link to infrastructural and ecosystem-level coordination (\textit{``\QuoteSilos{}''}; Appendix~\ref{appendix:C}). Reading rows top to bottom and columns left to right, \emph{technological} mechanisms operate through digital safeguards: cloud resilience, network modularity, and platform recoverability at the infrastructural scope \autocite{albert2000error,gao2016universal,panteli2017metrics,ibnehossain2020modeling}; monitoring, redundancy, and automated recovery at the organizational scope \autocite{linkov2013resilience,bjorck2015cyber,kuypers2018designing}; and shared infrastructure, interoperability, and distributed defense at the ecosystem scope \autocite{dupont2019cyberresilience,lostri2022shared,gillard2023efficient}. \emph{Managerial} mechanisms operate through human decision processes: infrastructure governance, access control, and escalation design at the infrastructural scope \autocite{boin2013resilient,calder2021iso,finma2023operational,nist2024csf}; incident response, learning routines, and decision protocols inside the firm \autocite{vogus2007organizational,weick2011managing,duchek2019organizational,boh2023building}; and cross-boundary governance, joint planning, and shared standards at the ecosystem scope \autocite{arnold2004informationsharing,baselcommittee2011basel,bcbs2021operational,european2022regulation}. \emph{Collaborative} mechanisms operate through coordinated action: operator--provider coordination, vendor response, and service restoration at the infrastructural scope \autocite{skopik2016problem,ecb2018tiber,hausken2020cyber}; internal cross-functional response and knowledge sharing inside the firm \autocite{lengnick2011developing,bhamra2011resilience,henfridsson2013generative}; and information sharing, collective response, and ecosystem adaptation across organizational boundaries \autocite{janssen2006network,enisa2017isacs,liu2022network,mahajan2022participatory}. Each cell names mechanisms that are well discussed within their own row or column. What is comparatively underdeveloped is the analysis of how the nine cells interact when an organization is actually disturbed---which is precisely where the framework directs attention.\\

Figure~\ref{fig:enactment_scope} also resonates with Figure~\ref{fig:triangle}: operational indicators apply most directly to the technological row \autocite{bruneau2003framework,francis2014metric,kuypers2018designing,gillard2023efficient}; design principles to the managerial row \autocite{boin2013resilient,calder2021iso,bcbs2021operational,nist2024csf}; benchmarks and warnings to the collaborative row \autocite{enisa2017isacs,ecb2018tiber,linkov2019fundamental,european2022regulation}. The framework also accommodates more demanding regimes in which organizations \emph{gain} structurally from exposure to disorder---the antifragile case in Taleb's sense \autocite{taleb2012antifragile}---by treating learning routines, reconfiguration capacity, and ecosystem-level information sharing as enacted, observable mechanisms rather than aspirational properties.
