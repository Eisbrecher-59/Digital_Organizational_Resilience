\section{The Enactment-Scope Framework}
\label{sec:Enactment-Scope}

The preceding analysis points to a basic tension in resilience research. Resilience is widely invoked as a desirable organizational capability, yet much of the literature still isolates only partial elements of it -- infrastructure robustness, organizational preparedness, or inter-organizational coordination -- without showing clearly how these elements combine to produce adaptive performance in digitally mediated environments \autocite{burnard2011organisational,boin2013resilient}. As a result, resilience is often discussed as a broad aspiration, yet remains difficult to operationalize, compare, or evaluate across organizational settings \autocite{linkov2013resilience,ibnehossain2020modeling}.\\

The more challenging task, then, is not simply to argue that resilience matters, but to demonstrate specific methods by which it becomes an observable and governable organizational capability. Figure~\ref{fig:triangle} illustrates this move from strategic aspiration to operationalization. Building on that logic, the Enactment-Scope framework specifies where resilience mechanisms operate and how they are realized across digitally mediated organizational systems. Operational indicators make adaptive mechanisms visible. Design principles define decision rights and structure coordination across organizational units. Benchmarks and warning systems align incentives and signal movement away from desired states. Together, these mechanisms translate resilience from rhetorical commitment into enacted organizational capability in practice \autocite{linkov2013resilience,boin2013resilient,ibnehossain2020modeling}.\\

That still leaves a question: where exactly do these mechanisms operate in digitally mediated organizational systems? Contemporary organizations function within socio-technical environments composed of digital infrastructures, organizational processes, and inter-organizational ecosystems \autocite{liu2022network,gillard2023efficient}. Resilience mechanisms operate across different socio-technical arrangements and scopes of coordination. The Enactment-Scope framework is crafted to seize that structure by organizing resilience mechanisms according to how they are enacted and the scopes within which they operate.

\subsection{Enactment of Resilience}

The first dimension of the framework concerns the \emph{enactment} of resilience. Resilience is not treated as an abstract attribute that organizations simply possess. It emerges through concrete mechanisms embedded in technological systems, organizational practices, and governance arrangements \autocite{duchek2020organizational,burnard2011organisational}. These mechanisms include technological safeguards like redundancy and automated recovery, organizational practices like incident-response routines and learning processes, and collaborative arrangements like information sharing and coordinated response across organizations \autocite{linkov2013resilience,dupont2019cyberresilience,gillard2023efficient}.\\
\\
Viewing resilience through enactment shifts attention from abstract claims to concrete socio-technical activity. Adaptive capacity arises from interactions among human actors, digital infrastructures, and institutional arrangements \autocite{liu2022network,dupont2019cyberresilience}. In digitally mediated environments, resilient performance depends on whether mechanisms are implemented, activated under disruption, and enable organizations to detect, respond to, and adapt as conditions evolve \autocite{duchek2020organizational,linkov2013resilience}. This makes enactment analytically important as it shifts focus from theoretical claims to practical implementations, thereby providing a clearer understanding of resilience in action.

\subsection{Scope of Resilience}

The second dimension concerns the \emph{scope} at which resilience mechanisms operate. Contemporary organizations rarely function in isolation, embedded in digitally interconnected environments composed of infrastructures, platforms, and collaborative networks \autocite{liu2022network,gillard2023efficient}. Resilience mechanisms may operate across multiple scopes of coordination rather than within a single organizational boundary.\\
\\
At the \emph{organizational} scope, resilience mechanisms are primarily internal to the firm, including governance structures, risk-management practices, and technological safeguards intended to maintain continuity of operations \autocite{duchek2020organizational,burnard2011organisational}. At the \emph{infrastructural} scope, resilience depends on the robustness and recoverability of shared digital systems like communication networks, cloud platforms, and critical information infrastructures \autocite{linkov2013resilience,ibnehossain2020modeling}. At the \emph{ecosystem} scope, resilience emerges through coordinated action across organizational boundaries, including information sharing, collaborative incident response, and collective governance arrangements \autocite{dupont2019cyberresilience,gillard2023efficient}.\\

Distinguishing these scopes clarifies that resilience in digital environments often depends on interactions among mechanisms operating across several layers of coordination \autocite{boin2013resilient,ibnehossain2020modeling}. An organization’s response to a cyber incident may depend not only on its internal detection and response capabilities, but also on the resilience of external infrastructures and the responsiveness of partner organizations across the wider ecosystem \autocite{dupont2019cyberresilience,gillard2023efficient}. Scope matters because the location of a resilience mechanism conditions both what it can achieve and what it depends on, highlighting the need for understanding interactions across different scopes.

\subsection{Integrating Enactment and Scope}

Bringing enactment and scope together provides a lens for examining how resilience mechanisms interact across socio-technical configurations. The framework shows how adaptive capacity emerges through the interaction of technological infrastructures, organizational practices, and ecosystem-level coordination mechanisms \autocite{duchek2020organizational,liu2022network,boin2013resilient}. It makes possible analysis of how resilience mechanisms reinforce one another, and where they generate dependencies, bottlenecks, blind spots, or misalignments across coordination scopes within digitally mediated organizational systems.\\

This is also where the framework departs from more generic multi-level accounts. It does not simply note that resilience involves several layers. It clarifies why resilience may appear strong at one scope while remaining fragile at another. An organization may possess well-developed internal response routines but depend on brittle infrastructures or unresponsive ecosystem partners. Conversely, robust infrastructures may not produce resilience when organizational routines are poorly aligned or cross-boundary coordination fails. The framework makes visible not only the distribution of resilience mechanisms but also the interdependencies through which resilient or non-resilient outcomes are produced.\\

The framework also helps explain why resilience is often difficult to operationalize in organizational contexts, primarily due to the complexity of integrating mechanisms across various socio-technical layers. Many resilience initiatives focus on mechanisms at only one scope -- internal preparedness, technological redundancy, or external coordination -- while neglecting how these mechanisms interact across socio-technical layers \autocite{burnard2011organisational,linkov2013resilience,boin2013resilient}. By linking mechanisms of enactment to the scopes within which they operate, the Enactment--Scope framework offers a more structured way to analyze how resilience arises from the interplay of organizational, technological, and ecosystem-level processes.\\

In that sense, the framework advances resilience research in two ways. The framework shows how adaptive capacity emerges from the interaction of technological infrastructures, organizational practices, and ecosystem-level coordination mechanisms \autocite{duchek2020organizational,liu2022network,boin2013resilient}. Second, it provides an analytical basis for developing operational measures that capture how resilience mechanisms function across digitally mediated organizational environments, thereby supporting empirical assessment, simulation-based analysis, and governance-oriented evaluation of digital resilience \autocite{linkov2013resilience,ibnehossain2020modeling}. Rather than treating resilience as an undifferentiated organizational virtue, the framework makes it possible to specify where adaptive capacity is located, how it is enacted, and why it succeeds or fails under conditions of disruption.
