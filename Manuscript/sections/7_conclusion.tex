\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:Conclusion}

Resilience, a buzzword in management and information systems research, often falls short of its promise, remaining more proclaimed than enacted. Organizations use it widely, but in practice, they face persistent ambiguity, methodological limitations, and weak empirical grounding. This ambiguity frequently translates into compliance-oriented practices that signal legitimacy without cultivating genuine adaptive capability.\\
\\
This review identified three core limitations in the current literature. First, construct clarity is lacking: resilience is often confused with robustness, agility, or redundancy, which obscures its unique mechanisms. Second, operationalization remains underdeveloped: organizations and scholars lack reliable indicators to capture adaptive performance during disruption. Third, cumulative evidence remains scarce: insights are fragmented across disciplines and contexts, preventing generalizable knowledge from emerging.\\
\\
To address these gaps, we propose a research agenda that conceptualizes resilience as a measurable socio-technical capability. Four directions are particularly important. First, operational indicators should render resilience observable through behavioral and computational markers. Second, integrative design principles must connect routines, infrastructures, and governance arrangements across organizational and technological layers. Third, benchmarks and early-warning systems are needed to align incentives and prevent the emergence of “metrics theater.” Finally, comparative and cumulative empirical research -- combining simulation, computational modeling, and field data -- can help establish transferable insights across organizational settings and levels of analysis.\\
\\
By following these directions, resilience is repositioned from a rhetorical aspiration to an enacted socio-technical capability. By advancing conceptual clarity, measurable indicators, and cumulative empirical knowledge, information systems research is uniquely positioned to transform resilience from a symbolic label into a tractable capability for navigating disruption in complex digital environments. More broadly, this review positions resilience as an emergent socio-technical capability arising from interactions among digital infrastructures, organizational practices, and institutional arrangements. By clarifying these mechanisms and identifying avenues for empirical and computational investigation, this study provides a foundation for cumulative theory development on resilience in digitally mediated organizational systems.
