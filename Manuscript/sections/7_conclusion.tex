\section{\MakeUppercase {Conclusion}}
\label{sec:Conclusion}
\renewcommand{\baselinestretch}{2.0}\normalsize


Resilience is a serious organizing concept in management and information systems research, increasingly central to how organizations articulate their response to disruption. Realizing its full promise requires extending the substantial work already done by regulators, practitioners, and scholars. In practice, organizations continue to navigate conceptual plurality, methodological challenges, and uneven empirical grounding. As a result, compliance-oriented practices—important as they are for accountability—capture only part of the underlying adaptive capability they are meant to support.\\
\\
This review identifies three priorities for the next stage of work. First, construct clarity can be sharpened: resilience is sometimes used alongside robustness, agility, or redundancy in ways that blur its distinctive mechanisms. Second, operationalization can be deepened: organizations and scholars are still developing reliable indicators to capture adaptive performance during disruption. Third, cumulative evidence can be strengthened: substantive insights have accumulated across disciplines and contexts, and the field is now positioned to integrate them into generalizable knowledge.\\
\\
To advance on these priorities, we propose a research agenda that conceptualizes resilience as a measurable socio-technical capability. Four directions are particularly important. First, operational indicators can render resilience observable through behavioral and computational markers that complement existing assessment instruments. Second, integrative design principles can connect routines, infrastructures, and governance arrangements across organizational and technological layers. Third, benchmarks and early-warning systems can align incentives and ensure that the structures organizations have put in place translate into adaptive capacity when pressure arrives. Finally, comparative and cumulative empirical research—combining simulation, computational modeling, and field data—can help establish transferable insights across organizational settings and levels of analysis.\\
\\
By following these directions, resilience can move from an organizing principle to an enacted socio-technical capability. By advancing conceptual clarity, measurable indicators, and cumulative empirical knowledge, information systems research is well positioned to help develop resilience into a tractable capability for navigating disruption in complex digital environments. More broadly, this review frames resilience as an emergent socio-technical capability arising from interactions among digital infrastructures, organizational practices, and institutional arrangements. By clarifying these mechanisms and identifying avenues for empirical and computational investigation, this study provides a foundation for cumulative theory development on resilience in digitally mediated organizational systems.
