% Abstract
\begin{abstract}
\renewcommand{\baselinestretch}{1.5}\normalsize
As organizations confront increasingly complex digital disruptions, resilience has become a central concern in management and information systems. Substantial advances have been achieved in policy, regulation, and organization science, yet translating these advances into observable, comparable, and actionable organizational capabilities remains uneven. Conceptual plurality, partial operationalization, and dispersed empirical evidence still limit the cumulative development of organizational foundations for resilience, which in turn slows the diffusion of actionable practices. This paper addresses these challenges through a structured, cross-disciplinary review of resilience research across management, information systems, and related fields. We identify three research priorities: sharpening construct clarity, developing resilience indicators capable of capturing adaptive performance and building cumulative evidence that travels across organizational contexts. Building on this diagnosis, we conceptualize digital organizational resilience as an enacted socio-technical capability linking organizational routines, technological infrastructures, and ecosystem interactions. We further outline a research agenda that emphasizes early-warning systems, integrative design principles, measurable behavioral indicators, and the importance of combining field data and simulations. This study contributes to theory development and provides a foundation for advancing observable and actionable forms of resilience in complex digital environments.
\end{abstract}
