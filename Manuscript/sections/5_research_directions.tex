\section{\MakeUppercase {Research Directions for Digital Organizational Resilience}}
\label{sec:RD}
\renewcommand{\baselinestretch}{2.0}\normalsize


The gap between resilience as policy commitment and resilience as enacted capability persists in part because research seldom specifies where adaptive mechanisms operate or how they depend on one another across a socio-technical organization. The Enactment--Scope framework addresses that omission by linking three enactment types---technological, managerial, and collaborative---to three coordination scopes---infrastructural, organizational, and ecosystem---and to the translation mechanisms through which stated commitment becomes observable practice. That structure converts the implementation gap diagnosed in Section~\ref{sec:RG} into empirically grounded questions about enactment. Cyber-intensive settings make those questions especially salient: field interviews (Appendix~\ref{appendix:C}) describe organizations that already maintain business continuity plans, SOC operations, and DORA-aligned testing regimes---yet rarely connect that industry backbone to governance, workforce planning, or board evaluation.

The framework converts the preceding diagnosis into three empirical questions. \emph{Enactment}: through what technological, managerial, and collaborative mechanisms does stated resilience become observable behavior before and during disruption? \emph{Scope}: at which coordination boundaries---digital infrastructure, organizational unit, inter-firm ecosystem---do those mechanisms succeed or fail in combination? \emph{Time}: does enacted capacity survive successive incidents, enhance, or on the contrary, erode once compliance cycles close?

Two complementary streams follow for IS and management research. \emph{Theoretical synthesis} must reconcile---without collapsing---the partly incompatible traditions reviewed in Section~\ref{sec:Background}: engineering recovery and reliability \autocite{bruneau2003framework,panteli2017metrics}, ecological reorganization \autocite{holling1973resilience,folke2010resilience}, psychological and collective adaptation \autocite{luthar2000construct,ungar2021modeling}, institutional governance \autocite{ostrom1990governing,lebel2006governance}, and complex-systems emergence \autocite{gao2016universal,krakovska2023resilience}. Management and IS work has imported these lineages selectively; what remains is an integrative vocabulary---enactment mechanisms, coordination scopes, translation pathways---that specifies design and accountability without definitional drift \autocite{hillmann2021organizational,woods2015four,duchek2019organizational}. \emph{Empirical transfer and validation} must move in a complementary direction: methods that already trace disruption, recovery, and cascade dynamics in engineering, public health, and complex systems \autocite{francis2014metric,cutter2010disaster,kruk2017building,dawson2011agentbased,aerts2018integrating} remain sparse in the management and cyber-risk cells of Table~\ref{tab:ResearchGaps}, where surveys and case studies still dominate. IS research is well placed to import and test them against digital organizational traces---SOC telemetry, recovery logs, exercise after-action reports, cross-firm sharing platforms---that organizations already generate but rarely analyze in order to connect to governance or board evaluation (Appendix~\ref{appendix:C}).

The subsections below follow three research streams---theory, measurement and modeling, and governance and design---each examined at the infrastructural, organizational, and ecosystem scopes of Figure~\ref{fig:enactment_scope}. Table~\ref{fig:resilience_interface_opportunity} adds a complementary prioritization lens. Its columns locate work across micro routines, meso infrastructures, macro institutions, and inter-organizational ecosystems; its rows distinguish six direction types---operational indicators, integrative frameworks, comparative and cumulative evidence, temporal and process markers, normative and value-based synthesis, and governance and incentives. Read together, the enactment--scope grid asks \emph{what} must be enacted and \emph{where}; the opportunity matrix asks \emph{which direction types} are empirically tractable at each analytical level today.

Three patterns from the matrix structure the agenda below. First, \emph{operational indicators} and \emph{temporal/process markers} are high-opportunity wherever digital traces are dense---micro routines, meso infrastructures, and ecosystem platforms---but remain only promising at macro institutional scope until cross-jurisdictional reporting converges \autocite{nist2024csf,meerow2016defining,allen2018quantifying,bcbs2021operational,european2022regulation}. Second, \emph{integrative frameworks} are most needed at meso infrastructures, macro institutions, and inter-organizational ecosystems, where partial literatures must be reconciled without collapsing into a single maturity score; at micro routines, validated indicators should precede further framework proliferation \autocite{hillmann2021organizational,duchek2019organizational}. Third, \emph{governance and incentives} advances are promising where dependency maps and sharing platforms already exist---meso and ecosystem columns---but stall at macro scope without comparable benchmarks and at micro scope when traces remain confined to security operations \autocite{skopik2016problem,rota2024}. \emph{Comparative and cumulative} work is similarly split: tractable across infrastructures and ecosystems, promising but harmonization-dependent at macro institutions, and low at micro routines until core indicators stabilize \autocite{francis2014metric,gillard2023efficient}. Simulation and exploratory modeling address cells the matrix marks as under-specified---especially temporal dynamics at meso scope and path-dependent reorganization that single-firm traces cannot yet observe \autocite{dawson2011agentbased,haasnoot2013dynamic,kwakkel2016comparing}.

\subsection{Theory}

The opportunity matrix rates \emph{integrative frameworks} as high-opportunity at meso infrastructures, macro institutions, and inter-organizational ecosystems---precisely where Section~\ref{sec:Background}'s divergent traditions must be reconciled without definitional drift \autocite{hillmann2021organizational,duchek2019organizational,woods2015four}. Before indicators or governance instruments can travel across settings, theory must settle what adaptive performance means, which mechanisms count as enactment, and how partly incompatible scientific traditions constrain design choices at each coordination scope. Three research directions follow---one per enactment--scope row of Figure~\ref{fig:enactment_scope}, aligned with the matrix columns above.

\textbf{Infrastructural scope (meso infrastructures).} Develop IS-informed theory of resilience when coordination is materially mediated by digital infrastructures. Build on generative entanglement between organizational routines and platform infrastructures \autocite{henfridsson2013generative}, coordination-design logic for unstable interdependence \autocite{galbraith1973designing}, and network-modularity arguments from cyber-resilience \autocite{albert2000error,panteli2017metrics,linkov2013resilience}. A concrete research direction is to specify when recovery of predefined functions, modular isolation, and reconfiguration under vendor or cloud dependency are competing success criteria---and which governance arrangements \autocite{boin2013resilient,dupont2019cyberresilience} make each criterion actionable. This directly addresses the matrix's high-opportunity \emph{integrative frameworks} cell at meso scope, where engineering recovery metrics and organizational governance logics still lack a shared operational vocabulary \autocite{bruneau2003framework,kuypers2018designing}.

\textbf{Organizational scope (micro routines).} Extend organization-theory accounts of adaptive capacity---high-reliability organizing and collective mindfulness \autocite{vogus2007organizational,weick2011managing}, dynamic capabilities for sensing and reconfiguration \autocite{teece2007explicating,duchek2019organizational,lengnick2011developing}, and the structural--cultural enablers emphasized in resilience management \autocite{bhamra2011resilience,burnard2011organisational,westrum2004typology}---into an integrative construct of \emph{digital organizational resilience}. The matrix marks \emph{integrative frameworks} at micro routines as low-opportunity until core indicators exist; the concrete research task is therefore to specify observable boundary conditions under which bounded-rational coordination \autocite{simon1997administrative,march1993organizations} under live disruption differs from preparedness, audit posture, and generic adaptability, and to show how technological, managerial, and collaborative mechanisms combine inside the firm without collapsing into a single maturity score \autocite{boh2023building,hepfer2022heterogeneity,hillmann2021organizational}. Normative and value-based synthesis at this level should follow indicator validation rather than precede it \autocite{woods2015four,duchek2019organizational}.

\textbf{Ecosystem scope (inter-organizational ecosystems).} Theorize collective resilience by linking institutional and IS accounts of cross-boundary coordination: polycentric and self-organizing governance \autocite{ostrom1990governing,lebel2006governance}, network-centric public-sector and platform arrangements \autocite{janssen2006network,liu2022network}, and information-sharing infrastructures for collective defense \autocite{arnold2004informationsharing,lostri2022shared,gillard2023efficient}. The concrete research task is to define emergent ecosystem resilience as dependence on cross-firm activation thresholds---when ISAC membership, shared standards, or platform interoperability translate into cooperative response under asymmetric threat, and when they remain declarative \autocite{enisa2017isacs,mahajan2022participatory,bjorck2015cyber,mermoud2019share}. This fills the matrix's high-opportunity \emph{integrative frameworks} and \emph{comparative and cumulative} cells at ecosystem scope, where collective defense infrastructures exist but lack cumulative theory of activated cooperation \autocite{gillard2023efficient,skopik2016problem}.

\subsection{Measurement and Modeling}

If resilience is enacted rather than declared, it must be measured where it leaves traces. Table~\ref{fig:resilience_interface_opportunity} prioritizes \emph{operational indicators} and \emph{temporal/process markers} at micro routines, meso infrastructures, and ecosystem levels---matching the trace-rich settings digital organizations already populate \autocite{francis2014metric,bruneau2003framework,kuypers2018designing}. Digital organizations now produce continuous security and behavioral logs---SOC and Security Information and Event Management (SIEM) telemetry, ticket histories, access and recovery timelines, coordination metadata---at volumes that make adaptive performance potentially observable, provided those traces are interpreted as evidence of enactment rather than as compliance artifacts alone. That opportunity invites a two-layer research strategy. A first layer applies quantitative, explainable methods from data science, computer science, and complex systems---event-sequence and survival modeling, network inference, perturbation--response analysis---that preserve visible assumptions for governance audiences \autocite{gao2016universal,dawson2011agentbased,krakovska2023resilience}. A second layer integrates these foundations with machine-learning pipelines embedded in explainable artificial intelligence (XAI) capable of rationalizing high-dimensional trace data without reducing adaptive performance to opaque risk scores \autocite{rane2024artificial,gillard2023efficient}. Commercial SIEM, observability, and security-analytics platforms---such as Splunk, Elastic Security, and Microsoft Sentinel---already provide much of this analytical infrastructure, yet they typically function as detection and compliance ``data telescopes'' rather than as instruments calibrated to observe and characterize resilience enactment. A related class of products---identity security posture platforms such as \href{https://saporo.io/}{Saporo}, a Swiss startup, that graph hybrid access dependencies and map attack paths across on-premises directories, cloud identities, and SaaS estates---sits more explicitly at the infrastructural--organizational interface. By combining complex-network analysis with machine-learning prioritization, these tools rank exploitable paths and remediation tasks; in effect, they generate operational indicators of posture and attack resistance. Yet their outputs, like SIEM findings, typically remain confined to tactical security workflows rather than being consolidated into governance rhythms or strategic decision cycles. The research task is therefore not only to import methods from adjacent fields, but to orient existing data-intelligence capabilities toward indicators that operators, boards, and regulators can interpret as evidence of adaptive performance. Three research directions follow, each targeting the matrix's highest-opportunity measurement cells at the corresponding scope.

\textbf{Infrastructural scope (meso infrastructures).} Import engineering and complex-systems methods---recovery-time objectives, network cascade models, perturbation--response and return-time dynamics---to measure how infrastructural interdependence shapes detection, reconfiguration, and service restoration during disruption \autocite{bruneau2003framework,ives1995measuring,gao2016universal,krakovska2023resilience,panteli2017metrics}. The matrix marks \emph{operational indicators} at meso scope as high-opportunity and \emph{temporal/process markers} as low-opportunity until longitudinal recovery traces accumulate at scale. Three research tasks follow: derive trace-based indicators of reconfiguration latency, cascade reach, and restoration drift relative to stated recovery objectives from dependency maps, failover logs, and hybrid topology metadata; calibrate cascade and perturbation--response models on those traces and test whether they predict return times observed in drills and live incidents \autocite{dawson2011agentbased,linkov2013resilience,ibnehossain2020modeling}; and evaluate whether recovery-orchestration platforms such as \href{https://www.resilio.io/}{Resilio}, which already productize hybrid-infrastructure measurement, can be recalibrated into governance-facing indicators of infrastructural enactment rather than tactical recovery metrics alone \autocite{francis2014metric,kuypers2018designing}.

\textbf{Organizational scope (micro routines).} Derive and validate micro-level indicators of adaptive performance---coordination latency across IT, risk, and compliance units; response variability under exercises; post-incident learning persistence; and internal coordination-network structure inferred from who routinely works with whom---from internal digital traces that field interviews show are collected but rarely linked to governance (\textit{``\QuoteQuantAmb{}''}; \textit{``\QuoteLearning{}''}; Appendix~\ref{appendix:C}) \autocite{skopik2016problem,barasa2018what,duchek2019organizational}. Both \emph{operational indicators} and \emph{temporal/process markers} are high-opportunity at micro scope---the densest trace environment in the matrix. Four research tasks follow: operationalize core indicators from event-sequence and handoff metadata in SOC and ticket systems, exercise logs, and post-incident documentation trails \autocite{kuypers2018designing}; infer internal coordination networks from collaboration traces that reveal durable ties of strength as well as fragile or overloaded handoffs; test construct validity by comparing trace-derived scores with exercise outcomes and successive incident response, and by distinguishing enacted adaptation from audit posture and generic preparedness \autocite{hepfer2022heterogeneity,westrum2004typology,boh2023building}; and evaluate whether coupling trace-based indicators to cross-functional governance rhythms---board reporting, workforce planning, and post-incident review---reduces the decision and learning gaps that interviews report when telemetry remains confined to security operations \autocite{francis2014metric,duchek2019organizational,rota2024}.

\textbf{Ecosystem scope (inter-organizational ecosystems).} Build comparative, cumulative metrics for cross-organizational coordination---information-sharing latency, joint-exercise performance, collective response activation---using platform logs, ISAC records, and sector-wide incident classifications now harmonized under DORA and Basel operational-resilience principles \autocite{bcbs2021operational,european2022regulation,enisa2017isacs,mahajan2022participatory,ecb2018tiber}. The matrix marks \emph{operational indicators} at ecosystem scope as high-opportunity and \emph{comparative and cumulative} evidence as low-opportunity until shared baselines stabilize across participants \autocite{gillard2023efficient,mermoud2019share}. Commercial offerings such as \href{https://ccrc.nl/chain-cyber-crisis-exercises/}{CCRC Chain}, which convenes joint cyber-crisis simulations across supplier and partner boundaries in the Netherlands \autocite{ccrc2024chain,european2022regulation,ecb2018tiber}, sit more naturally at this scope: they stress-test how disruption propagates across third-party boundaries and, in effect, generate indicators of collective exposure \autocite{dupont2019cyberresilience,hausken2020cyber,shen2024understanding,bcbs2021operational}---yet rarely consolidate those findings into multi-party governance or sector-wide learning routines \autocite{skopik2016problem,mahajan2022participatory}. Two research tasks follow: operationalize and validate comparative indicators from platform logs, ISAC records, and harmonized incident classifications, testing whether they distinguish declarative membership from activated cooperation under asymmetric threat \autocite{enisa2017isacs,mahajan2022participatory,gillard2023efficient,mermoud2019share}; and evaluate whether exercise and telemetry outputs feed multi-party governance rhythms---sector briefings, supplier escalation, shared after-action learning---rather than remaining confined to bilateral security workflows \autocite{arnold2004informationsharing,lostri2022shared,skopik2016problem}.

Trace-based measurement nonetheless leaves important matrix cells under-specified. Conventional risk-management toolkits estimate exposure, loss, and control effectiveness under assumed threat models; they ask how severe outcomes might be and how probable they are. Resilience analysis asks a different question: under sustained or repeated disruption, does coordinated adaptive capacity stabilize, fragment, or reorganize---and along which pathways? That question is dynamic and path-dependent. It cannot be answered from audit snapshots or from correlational analysis on a single organizational history alone \autocite{woods2015four,duchek2019organizational,folke2004regime,holling1973resilience}. Simulation---especially agent-based and other generative models---therefore complements observational traces in the cells where the matrix marks \emph{temporal/process markers} or \emph{integrative frameworks} as low-opportunity despite their theoretical importance \autocite{dawson2011agentbased,pumpuni2017resilience,aerts2018integrating,helbing2012agent}. Where digital logs reveal what did happen inside one firm or platform, simulation explores counterfactual ensembles of how technological, managerial, and collaborative mechanisms interact across infrastructural, organizational, and ecosystem boundaries before live disruption \autocite{gao2016universal,krakovska2023resilience,gillard2023efficient}. Under deep uncertainty about adversarial adaptation, vendor coupling, and cross-unit coordination, the aim is not point prediction but \emph{scenario discovery}: identifying recurrent outcome regimes, the structural levers that sort configurations among them, and the observation horizons at which diverging pathways become distinguishable \autocite{kwakkel2016comparing,haasnoot2013dynamic,lempert2019robust}. Generative work on adversarial cooperation dynamics illustrates why this matters for resilience beyond risk scoring: endpoint cooperation can mask qualitatively different trajectories---ongoing reorganization versus stabilized containment---and the futures hardest to anticipate from partial evidence are often those heading toward systemic degradation rather than toward quiet recovery \autocite{folke2004regime,walker2004resilience}. Monitoring design must therefore pair defender-side traces with threat-side proxies and concentrate on medium-horizon windows when pathways are actively separating, rather than assume that more data or longer observation automatically improves foresight \autocite{haasnoot2013dynamic,francis2014metric}.

Four simulation-linked research tasks follow, each targeting an under-specified matrix intersection. First, embed enactment--scope mechanisms in generative models that couple infrastructure cascades, internal coordination latencies, and cross-firm sharing rules---addressing low-opportunity \emph{temporal/process markers} at meso scope---and map where combined failures produce qualitatively different recovery trajectories rather than scalar risk scores \autocite{dawson2011agentbased,linkov2013resilience,arnold2004informationsharing}. Second, use exploratory modeling and stress testing to identify high-leverage structural levers---modularity, escalation design, sharing activation thresholds---that partition the outcome space under uncertainty \autocite{cutter2010disaster,ibnehossain2020modeling,skopik2016problem}. Third, calibrate simulation outputs against the trace-based indicators developed above and against exercise data (including chain-wide simulations such as CCRC Chain), treating models as governance-facing scenario laboratories rather than opaque forecast engines \autocite{ccrc2024chain,ecb2018tiber,francis2014metric,gillard2023efficient}. Fourth, identify observation horizons and monitoring signposts at which diverging resilience pathways become distinguishable before outcomes stabilize---building the temporal evidence base the matrix currently lacks at meso and macro scope \autocite{haasnoot2013dynamic,francis2014metric,kwakkel2016comparing,nist2024csf,meerow2016defining}.

\subsection{Governance and Design}

The opportunity matrix locates \emph{governance and incentives} as promising at meso infrastructures and inter-organizational ecosystems, but low at macro institutions until comparable benchmarks exist and at micro routines until indicators connect traces to decision rights \autocite{skopik2016problem,rota2024,bcbs2021operational,nist2024csf}. Governance and design translate observed adaptive performance into decision rights, escalation structure, and aligned incentives \autocite{boin2013resilient,calder2021iso,bcbs2021operational,nist2024csf,linkov2019fundamental}. Three research directions follow, each aligned with the matrix's most tractable governance cells at the corresponding enactment--scope level.

\textbf{Infrastructural scope (meso infrastructures).} Specify design principles for infrastructure governance under stress---access control, vendor escalation, operator--provider coordination, governance of critical software updates, service-restoration priorities---and evaluate them against benchmarks derived from recovery logs and third-party dependency maps \autocite{finma2023operational,hausken2020cyber,ecb2018tiber,skopik2016problem}. The matrix marks this cell as promising rather than fully tractable: governance instruments exist, but benchmark harmonization across providers and jurisdictions remains incomplete \autocite{bcbs2021operational,european2022regulation}. Two research tasks follow: articulate those principles for infrastructure governance under stress; and evaluate them against benchmarks derived from recovery logs, third-party dependency maps, and the trace-based indicators developed above \autocite{finma2023operational,hausken2020cyber,ecb2018tiber,skopik2016problem,francis2014metric,kuypers2018designing}.

\textbf{Organizational scope (micro routines).} Design governance arrangements that couple siloed units---IT, risk, compliance, operations---through shared indicators, rehearsal routines, and explicit decision protocols, rather than folding resilience into existing committees without cross-functional mandate (\textit{``\QuoteSilos{}''}; Appendix~\ref{appendix:C}) \autocite{weick2011managing,bhamra2011resilience,duchek2019organizational,rota2024}. The matrix rates \emph{governance and incentives} at micro scope as low-opportunity until the operational indicators above reach board-facing validity; research should therefore test governance designs jointly with trace-based measurement rather than as standalone policy templates. Three research tasks follow: design those cross-functional arrangements; evaluate them against the trace-based indicators developed above; and test whether simulation-derived scenario laboratories reveal where proposed arrangements fail under cross-unit stress \autocite{weick2011managing,bhamra2011resilience,duchek2019organizational,rota2024,gillard2023efficient}.

\textbf{Ecosystem scope (inter-organizational ecosystems).} Develop warning systems and incentive structures that sustain cross-firm cooperation when activation delays, trust asymmetries, and platform-design frictions would otherwise block collective response---drawing on modular security platforms, bug-bounty governance, disaster-response information-sharing design, and behavioral accounts of why ISAC participation often underutilizes sharing despite its collective benefits \autocite{maillart2017bugbounty,brauer2008compartmental,arnold2004informationsharing,lostri2022shared,mermoud2019share}. This addresses the matrix's promising \emph{governance and incentives} cell at ecosystem scope, where sharing infrastructures exist but activation remains fragile \autocite{mermoud2019share,gillard2023efficient}. Two research tasks follow: develop those warning systems and incentive structures for sustained cross-firm cooperation; and evaluate them against trace-based indicators, exercise outputs, and simulation-derived scenario findings \autocite{maillart2017bugbounty,brauer2008compartmental,arnold2004informationsharing,lostri2022shared,gillard2023efficient,mermoud2019share}.

\subsection{Toward an Integrative Enactment-Scope Research Agenda}

Taken together, the agenda above is cumulative rather than modular. Table~\ref{fig:resilience_interface_opportunity} and Figure~\ref{fig:enactment_scope} jointly specify where to invest next: theory concentrates on high-opportunity \emph{integrative frameworks} at meso, macro, and ecosystem levels; measurement prioritizes \emph{operational indicators} and \emph{temporal/process markers} where traces are densest; simulation fills cells those rows still leave under-specified; and governance advances where \emph{governance and incentives} is already promising---meso and ecosystem---once indicators and benchmarks make adaptive performance legible to decision-makers \autocite{francis2014metric,skopik2016problem,rota2024}. Theory at each coordination scope clarifies what counts as enacted adaptive performance and which mechanisms should be measured or governed. Measurement and modeling operationalize that performance from digital traces, orient commercial telemetry toward governance-facing indicators, and complement observational work with generative simulation under deep uncertainty. Governance and design then couple those indicators, exercises, and scenario findings to decision rights, escalation, and incentives---addressing the field evidence that operational data rarely reach board or cross-functional review (Appendix~\ref{appendix:C}). Read across both tables, resilience becomes an enacted, distributed, and revisable socio-technical capability when theory, evidence, simulation, and governance advance together at each scope---not when organizations optimize a single cell in isolation \autocite{weick2011managing,woods2015four,teece2007explicating}. For IS research, that means programs that combine construct synthesis, trace-based quantification, exploratory simulation, and field-validated governance design---closing the gap between articulated commitment and observable adaptive performance under persistent uncertainty \autocite{aerts2018integrating,gillard2023efficient,kalisch2024neurobiology}. Section~\ref{sec:ImplicationsResearch} takes up what that cumulative program implies for how resilience should be understood and evaluated in practice.
