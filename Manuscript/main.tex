% Abstract
\begin{abstract}
\renewcommand{\baselinestretch}{1.5}\normalsize
As organizations confront increasingly complex digital disruptions, resilience has become a central concern in management and information systems. Despite substantial advances in policy, regulation, and organization science, resilience remains widely declared yet hard to evidence: it is typically certified at a point in time---through audits, maturity scores, and plan certifications---rather than traced as adaptive performance across successive disruptions. We combine a structured, cross-disciplinary review with an exploratory field study of senior cybersecurity and risk professionals. Together they expose three interlocking deficits: conceptual plurality without operational convergence, limited empirical validation of adaptive performance, and methodological fragmentation across domains. To organize research across these deficits, we develop the Enactment--Scope framework---an analytical device for structuring inquiry. It locates \emph{how} resilience is enacted---through technological, managerial, and collaborative mechanisms---and \emph{where} those enactments must be studied---across infrastructural, organizational, and ecosystem scopes---and reframes resilience as an enacted, observable socio-technical capability. On this basis we set out a cumulative research path: fixing the construct and trace-based indicators, validating and calibrating them against incident, exercise, and simulation data, and binding them to governance---including the way governance itself reconfigures under disruption---so that adaptive performance can be measured, governed, and accumulated into evidence that travels across contexts. The paper contributes an integrative framework, a measurement-oriented research agenda, and a foundation for observable and actionable resilience in digitally mediated environments.
\end{abstract}
